# Penguin

Status: **abandoned**.

This note is longer than the others because the question "why not penguins?" comes up
roughly every six weeks, always from someone who has just heard the pitch and is very
excited about it, and it is faster to send them this than to tell the story again.

## The pitch

Cold brew, except the cold happens inside the animal. A bean fermented at low temperature
should extract differently, and **"naturally cold-processed"** is one of the best lines
anyone has ever put on a bag of coffee.

Everyone in the room loved it. That should have been the second warning. The first warning
was that the person who proposed it did not know what penguins eat.

## Phase 1 — The arrangement

A wildlife park agreed to a trial with fourteen Humboldt penguins.
[[Legal/Regulatory]] signed off on one condition: no animal is ever fed anything against
its will.

We agreed to that condition immediately and without discussion. We did not yet understand
that it would turn out to be the entire result of the experiment.

## Phase 2 — The feeding trial

| Day | Cherry offered | Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | In the standard feed trays | Ignored completely |
| 2 | Alongside fish | The fish went. The cherries stayed. |
| 3 | Scattered at the waterline | One bird moved a cherry. We logged this as progress. It had moved it out of the way. |
| 4–9 | Various | Nothing. One bird stood on a cherry for some time, with what the log records as "intent". |
| 10 | Hand-offered by a keeper | The keeper was bitten. Not hard. Pointedly. |

Eleven of fourteen birds never touched a coffee cherry at any point in the trial.

This was not a surprise to anyone who had read anything. Penguins are carnivores; they eat
fish, squid and krill. The hope had been that a ripe coffee cherry is sweet, sticky and
roughly the size and colour of something a bird might investigate.

The hope was not scientific. The hope was that we had already designed the packaging.

## Phase 3 — The three that did

By day eleven, three birds had eaten cherries. Our log calls them P4, P7 and P11. The park
staff had their own names for these three, and asked us not to publish them, which in
hindsight was all the information we needed.

A coffee cherry contains caffeine. A Humboldt penguin weighs about four kilograms and has,
as far as anyone has been able to establish, no evolutionary exposure to caffeine of any
kind.

The full account is **INC-7** in [[Legal/Incidents]]. The short version:

- **P4** swam. Continuously. For a period the park described, in writing, as "not typical".
- **P7** vocalised. At length, and at a volume that brought a keeper in from a different
  enclosure to see what had happened.
- **P11** got out. Nobody has ever established how. The gate was closed afterwards. It had
  also been closed before.

No bird came to any harm. The park vet confirmed this twice, in writing, because we asked
twice, in writing.

But the welfare question had become unanswerable, and it answered itself: we were
administering a stimulant to an animal with no use for it, in order to produce a coffee we
had not yet tasted. That is the whole of ADR-2 in [[Meetings/Decisions]], and we were on
the wrong side of it.

## Phase 4 — The tasting

We did eventually taste it. Four hundred grams, green, from three birds, retrieved under
conditions [[Operations/Retrieval]] was simply never written for — the four-hour wash
limit assumes an animal that can be approached, and the relevant animals had by that point
formed opinions.

The panel protocol in [[Operations/Quality-Control]] is blind. Three cuppers, no animal
named, no exceptions.

Somebody told them.

Two of the three declined to taste it at all. The third tasted it, recorded **"clean,
slightly saline, unremarkable"**, and then also declined to continue. All three asked to be
left off the panel for any future work on this line.

It remains the only time in company history that a cupping panel has resigned from a
product rather than scored it.

"Slightly saline" is the only tasting data this line ever produced. It does not appear in
[[Brand/Tasting-Notes]], because we have no approved descriptor for it and no desire to
invent one.

## Phase 5 — The control batch

This is the part that actually ended the project.

We ran a control: same farm, same lot, soaked in cold salt water at 4 °C for the same
duration as the trial, with no penguin involved at any stage. Three cuppers, none of whom
knew which cup was which.

The results were indistinguishable. One cupper mildly preferred the control.

The cold had been doing the work. The salt water had been doing the rest. The penguin had
been a logistics cost wearing a story.

## Conclusion

The pitch was never about the penguin. It was about the temperature, and we are perfectly
capable of producing a temperature. We own a refrigerator. It has never once got out.

Logged as **ADR-5** in [[Meetings/Decisions]]. Do not reopen without a penguin that eats
fruit, which is to say: do not reopen.

## Where this went instead

In the same week the control results came back, someone who had been quietly pointing at a
different animal for about a month pointed at it again, harder. That animal was the
[[Animals/Capybara]].

The capybara eats the coffee cherry without being asked twice. It sits in water
voluntarily. It tolerates being approached. It has never been described in any document as
having "intent". Nothing belonging to the capybara has ever got out.

It has produced roughly 70% of company revenue ever since, and it is the reason this
company still exists. See [[Market/Pricing]] for what that subsidises.

## The lesson, for the next person with a pitch

**Pick the animal that wants the job.**

Everything else in this note is a consequence of not doing that.
