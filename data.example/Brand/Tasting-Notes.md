# Tasting Notes

Writing tasting notes for this product is harder than making it.

A normal coffee note can reach for earthy, funky, barnyard, gamey, animal. Those words are
in every cupper's vocabulary and all of them are, for us, catastrophic.

## Approved descriptors

| Line | Note |
|---|---|
| Still Waters (capybara) | Soft, hazelnut, clean finish, light sweetness |
| Night Harvest (bat) | Floral, honeyed, bright, jasmine |
| Slow Press (sloth) | Deep, winey, dried fruit, long finish |
| Grey Reserve (elephant) | Cocoa, tamarind, no bitterness, syrupy |
| Kaldi's Own (goat) | Grassy, bright, simple |

## Banned words

Every one of these has appeared in an internal cupping sheet and none may leave the
building: earthy, barnyard, funky, gamey, animal, musky, pungent, compost, rich in
character.

"Compost" is the honest note on a bad sloth batch. It appears in
[[Operations/Quality-Control]] as a class C defect and nowhere else.

## The standing rule

**A tasting note describes the cup, not the journey.**

Marketing has tested the opposite twice. Both campaigns led with the mechanism, both
produced excellent engagement and a drop in conversion, which is exactly the pattern in
[[Market/Customer-Research]]: people love hearing about this and buy in spite of it, not
because of it.

The one permitted mechanism phrase is "wild-fermented", which is true of every line and
specific to none.
